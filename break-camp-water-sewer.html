Break Camp: Water, Sewer, and Tank Treatment

This guide covers dumping and flushing all three holding tanks, recharging them with the Kleen Tank solution, disconnecting and storing hoses, and the final walkaround. It follows the same order as the Tanks and Water Disconnect sections of the Camp Departure checklist in the Rollout app.

Full hookup siteThe sewer hose is already connected from setup. Skip to Phase 2. City water stays connected until the tanks are recharged.
Dump station on the way outStart at Phase 1. Fill a 3 to 5 gallon water container at the site spigot before you leave the site.
CORRECTION TO THE OWNER'S GUIDE The Alliance Owner's Guide has you remove the RV outlet cap and connect the hose at the RV before the other end reaches the dump inlet. That leaves the outlet open while you route the hose. This guide reverses the order: connect at the dump inlet first, route the hose, and expose the RV outlet only when the receiving end is secured and the main dump valve is closed.
00

Before You Start

  • Disposable safety gloves on
  • Sewer hose, flush and wash hose (non-potable), and blue potable hose on hand. The flush and wash hose is never used for drinking water.
  • Kleen Tank jug shaken, plus an 8 oz measuring cup
  • A marked 1 gallon jug for measuring priming water
  • Dump station only: 3 to 5 gallon water container filled at the site spigot
  • Bucket or towel for drips
  • Three tank valves located on the driver side underbelly, aft of the rear tires: black, gray 1, and gray 2. Main dump valve located.
  • Black flush inlet located on the driver side convenience center
  • Pressure regulator in place. Never exceed 50 PSI into the black flush inlet.
01

Phase 1: Connect the Sewer Hose (Dump Station)

At a full hookup site the hose is already connected from setup. Confirm both ends are tight and the main dump valve is open, then go to Phase 2.

  1. Confirm the main dump valve is closed CRITICALPush the handle fully in. It should feel solid.
  2. Connect at the dump inlet firstAttach one end of the sewer hose to the dump station inlet, hand tight and sealed. Tug gently to confirm it does not slip. Lay the hose back to the RV with a continuous downhill fall to the inlet.
  3. Connect at the RV outletRemove the outlet cap (turn counterclockwise, then pull down) and set it where it will not roll away. Attach the hose, seat it fully, and tug to confirm.
  4. Open the main dump valveThe path from tanks to inlet is now complete and sealed.
02

Phase 2: Wet the Hose, Dump and Flush Black

  1. Connect the flush and wash hoseConnect it to the black flush inlet on the trailer.
    • Full hookup: the other end is already on the second leg of the Y-splitter at the spigot, with its ballcock closed.
    • Dump station: connect the other end to the station's rinse spigot. Leave the spigot off.
  2. Dump gray 1 to wet the hoseOpen the gray 1 valve fully. When the flow stops, push it fully closed. Wetting the hose first reduces splashing and sticking when the black tank goes.
  3. Dump the black tankPull the black valve fully open. Flow runs about 3 to 5 minutes. Stay with it and listen. Silence or only drips means the tank is empty.
  4. Flush the black tank DO NOT SKIPLeave the black valve open. Open the black flush ballcock at the Y (or the dump station spigot). Flush about 2 to 5 minutes until the discharge runs clear.
  5. Shut off the flush water CRITICALClose the black flush ballcock at the Y (or the spigot). Let the tank finish draining, then push the black valve fully closed.
  6. Disconnect the flush and wash hoseRemove it from the black flush inlet. Keep it away from the blue potable hose.
WARNING Never run flush water with the black valve closed. The tank can overfill and back up into the toilet.
03

Phase 3: Dump Gray 2 and Close Up

  1. Dump gray 2 last to rinse the hoseOpen the gray 2 valve fully. Flow runs about 2 to 3 minutes. The soapy gray water carries residue out of the hose. When the flow stops, push the valve fully closed.
  2. Confirm all three tank valves are closed CRITICALBlack, gray 1, and gray 2. Push each handle straight in until it is solid.
  3. Close the main dump valve CRITICAL
04

Phase 4: Disconnect, Drain, and Store the Sewer Hose

  1. Disconnect at the RVLoosen the hose by hand (counterclockwise) with a bucket or towel ready for drips.
  2. Replace the outlet cap immediatelyScrew it on clockwise until it is hand tight and fully seated.
  3. Drain the hoseRaise the RV end 12 to 18 inches and walk it toward the inlet so the contents run downhill. At the inlet, lift the end as high as you can reach and hold 30 to 60 seconds until the flow stops.
  4. Rinse the hose interiorRun water from the flush and wash hose through the sewer hose for 30 to 60 seconds, until the outflow runs clear.
  5. Disconnect at the inletRemove the hose from the dump inlet.
  6. Store the sewer hoseCoil it loosely in a 3 to 4 foot loop with no kinks. Let it air dry when time allows, then stow it in its own compartment. Never store it outside the RV or with the blue potable hose.
  7. Gloves off, hands washed CRITICALDo this before handling anything inside the RV.
05

Phase 5: Recharge the Tanks (Kleen Tank)

With all valves closed, dose each tank and add travel priming water. Road motion spreads the solution over the tank walls and sensors on the drive.

  1. Shake the jugThe Calgon settles. Shake before every use.
  2. Dose each tank, 8 oz
    • Black: down the toilet
    • Galley gray: down the kitchen sink
    • Bath gray: down the shower drain
  3. Add travel priming waterUse the marked gallon jug.
    • Black: 3 gallons through the toilet
    • Each gray tank: 1 to 2 gallons through its drain
  4. Choose the water source
    • Full hookup: use city water. It is still connected at this point.
    • Dump station: prime the black tank from the container you filled at the site. Run a short amount from the fresh tank, with the pump on, for the gray tanks.
    • Boondocking next: prime the black tank with 5 gallons and skip the gray tanks to protect fresh water.
  5. Cap and secure the jugStore it upright and wedged for travel, away from food.

06

Phase 6: Disconnect City Water

Full hookup only. City water stays connected until the tanks are recharged.

  1. Confirm the water pump is off
  2. Close the spigot
  3. Bleed the pressureOpen the potable ballcock at the Y to relieve line pressure.
  4. Disconnect the braided jumper at the trailer inletTurn counterclockwise by hand over a bucket or towel.
  5. Remove and drain the filter
  6. Disconnect and drain the blue potable hose
  7. Drain the flush and wash hose
  8. Remove the regulator and Y-splitter from the spigot
  9. Stow all hoses and fittingsThe blue potable hose, the flush and wash hose, and the sewer hose each go in separate places. None of them touch.
07

Post-Dump Inspection

CHECK BEFORE YOU PULL OUT
  • Black, gray 1, gray 2, and the main dump valve each pushed fully in and solid
  • Outlet cap hand tight and does not turn
  • No drips around the outlet. Drips with all valves closed point to a worn cap seal.
  • All hoses stowed in separate compartments. None left on the ground or outside the RV.
  • Kleen Tank jug capped and secured
  • Optional: log the dump date, tank levels, and any issues
08

On Arrival at the Next Camp

The tanks already hold their dose from departure. Arrival priming adds the rest of the water they need to work.

  1. Connect shore waterFollow the Water section of the Arrival checklist.
  2. Prime the black tank firstBefore anyone uses the toilet, add 2 to 3 more gallons.
  3. Prime the gray tanksAdd 2 to 3 gallons to each, or let normal use fill them.
NOTE If you dumped at a station after leaving camp and did not recharge, add the 8 oz dose to each tank now.
09

Mid-Trip Dumps and Special Cases

  • When to dump: Dump when a tank is 90 to 100 percent full where practical. A full tank drains with more force and carries solids out. If you must dump early, add water first to raise the level.
  • After a mid-trip dump: Close the valves, add 8 oz to each emptied tank, and prime with 3 to 5 gallons, since you are not traveling to slosh it.
  • Boondocking or no shore water: Before departing, prime the black tank with 5 gallons from the dump station water source. Skip arrival priming on the gray tanks to protect fresh water.
  • Sensor misreads or odor: Increase the dose in the affected tank first. The galley tank is the most likely, because of grease.
WARNING Use the Kleen Tank solution only. Do not combine it with enzyme, bacteria, or digesting treatments. Those leave a sticky residue that fouls sensors and does not fully drain.
10

Valve Management During a Stay

  • For travel: All three tank valves and the main dump valve are closed. Never tow with any valve open.
  • Black valve: Keep it closed for the whole stay. Open it only to dump. A black tank left open drains the liquid and leaves solids behind.
  • Gray valves, default: Keep them closed and open them only to dump. A closed tank holds the Kleen Tank solution and builds the volume needed for a strong drain.
  • Gray valves, heavy use alternative: During back-to-back showers or heavy cleanup on a sewer hookup, a gray valve may be left open briefly. This needs a p-trap or loop in the sewer hose to block sewer gas, and sewer flies can still get past a trap. Close the valve within 30 minutes of heavy use ending.

Appendix: Mixing the Kleen Tank Solution

One gallon batch:

IngredientAmountHow to measure
Calgon bath powder (water-softening bath soak)8 ozBy weight on a kitchen scale, or one heaping cup
Pine-Sol or similar pine oil cleaner40 fl ozBy volume (5 cups)
Warm waterabout 10 cupsFill to the top of the jug
  1. Check the Calgon labelIt must say to add it to bathwater. A body or dusting powder (talc or cornstarch) does not dissolve and settles as sludge on the sensors. Do not use it.
  2. Add the powder firstPut the 8 oz of Calgon in the empty jug. Powder clumps if it lands on liquid.
  3. Add the Pine-Sol40 fl oz.
  4. Add warm water slowlyPause to let the foam settle, then fill to the top.
  5. Cap and shake gently
  6. Let it sit overnightShake again in the morning so the powder fully dissolves.
  7. Label the jug"HOLDING TANK SOLUTION, DO NOT DRINK"

Critical Safety Notes

  • One valve at a time. Never open more than one tank valve at once. The only exception is the black valve staying open while you flush.
  • Black valve timing. Close it only when the tank is empty. If unsure, give it another 30 to 60 seconds. Solids caught in the gate cause odor and seal failure.
  • Outlet exposure. Never leave the RV outlet open without a receiving hose in place. Remove the cap only with the main dump valve closed.
  • Valve handles. Push and pull straight along the rod, never sideways. The aluminum rods bend, and a bent rod will not seal.
  • Stiff valves. Apply silicone lubricant where the rod enters the valve body and cycle the valve several times. Never use WD-40 or mineral oil. Repeat every 2 to 3 years. If a valve stays stiff, have it serviced before the next dump.
  • Hose separation. The sewer hose, the flush and wash hose, and the blue potable hose never share storage or touch. Replace the sewer hose every 5 to 7 years, or at once if it shows cracks, splits, or soft spots.
  • Tank treatment. Kleen Tank solution only. No enzyme, digester, or caustic additives. Mix it with good ventilation, keep it off skin and out of eyes, and store it away from food.
